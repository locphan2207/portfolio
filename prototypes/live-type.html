01 · The effect

Letters that lean in

Each glyph reads its distance to the pointer and resolves its own weight. Move across the name slowly: the response is a travelling swell, not a hover state. Nothing here is an image or a canvas — it is the live font, one font-variation-settings per letter.

02 · The axis audit

The idea assumes WONK and SOFT are already paid for. They are not. assets/fonts/fraunces-latin.woff2 carries two axes — opsz and wght — and the other two were instanced out when the subset was made. Below, every axis is live from the Google build so you can see what each one actually buys.

03 · The problem nobody mentions

Weight changes width

This is what sinks a naive build. Going from 400 to 900 makes “Loc Phan” 12.7% wider — about 50px at hero size. Animate weight alone and the line breathes, the letters shove each other, and every element after it on the line moves. All three lanes below run the same effect off the same horizontal position, so they always sit at the same weight and the only thing being compared is the discipline. Watch the dashed rule at the resting width.

The trick in lane B. Weight widens a glyph; optical size narrows it, so the two can be made to cancel. The slope that cancels them is not a constant — it moves with the face, the size and the string — so the page measures it at boot instead of hard-coding one: it bisects for the opsz that brings the line at peak weight back to its resting width, then walks that line. Move the whole word to 900 along it and the width holds to under 0.1%, against +10.4% for weight alone.
Why lane C still exists. A pointer falloff does not move every letter at once, and the slope only cancels for the word as a whole — so under a real sweep lane B still drifts about +2%. Locking each box at its resting advance is what takes that to zero. The two work together: locking alone would hold the line while letters fat enough to overflow their boxes collided with their neighbours, and the iso-width path is what keeps them from overflowing — mean glyph growth at peak drops from 10.3% to 2.9%, and only P still reaches past its box at all.
Measured every number here is read off the live font at the size it renders Locking each box is measured once at rest and re-measured on resize and on fonts.ready Kerning splitting into spans drops kerning pairs; the line carries −.03em tracking to compensate

04 · The related note

On a phone, the colour never arrives

The hero's gradient is revealed by a mask that chases the pointer, so with no pointer the flat copy stays on top and the colour never shows. The arrival sweep hides this on the hero — but it only runs on #heroTitle, so the contact heading, which uses the same mechanism, is flat forever on touch. Under html.calm the mask is disabled outright and the gradient never shows anywhere.

Where this lands

Build it on the axes you already paid for

The effect is worth having and costs nothing to ship. The premise underneath it was wrong in a useful direction: the two axes the idea wanted are the two that are missing, and the two that are already on the page are the better pair anyway.

Ship

wght + opsz

Both already in the file, both continuous across every glyph, and together they walk the iso-width path that keeps the line from breathing. Zero new bytes. This is the whole effect.

Free, but small

WONK, if you re-subset

Restoring it is 212 bytes cheaper than the file you serve today. But it snaps rather than interpolates and only touches h, m, n, s and & — in “Loc Phan” that is two letters. Worth folding into the next font rebuild; not worth one on its own.

Skip

SOFT

The nicest axis to animate and the only one with a real price: +53 KB, nearly doubling the display font, for a softening most visitors will never consciously see. Not against a site that ships three self-hosted faces and no framework.

Independently of idea 03: the [data-fill] dead spot in section 04 is a live bug on the page today, not a design question. The contact heading never shows its gradient on a touch device, and no heading shows it under reduced motion. That one is worth fixing whether or not any of this ships.

Prototype only — nothing here is wired into index.html. Palette, type roles and the reduce-motion contract are pulled from assets/css/site.css so the demo reads true. Fraunces is served from Google Fonts with all four axes, which is the one thing that differs from production — and the point of section 02.
